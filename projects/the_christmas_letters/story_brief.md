## Premise / idea

Six years after Eleanor Cole threw Valentina Reyes out of the Cole mansion on Christmas night, Val returns as the hired decorator for the family's Christmas Eve engagement gala. Nate Cole, the heir she once loved, is engaged to Madison Pierce, a glamorous socialite who wants half of Cole Hotels in the prenup. Val does not know that Eleanor paid Walter Briggs, the mansion's porter, to intercept all 72 letters Val wrote to Nate over six years, in both directions, and that Eleanor forged a goodbye letter signed with Val's name but missing the small star Val always draws beside her signature. As Val decorates the mansion, she finds one stolen letter, a red cookie tin, and an ally in June Alvarez, the cook. Nate begins to doubt his mother's story, Madison's business motives surface, and Walter's guilt cracks open. On Christmas Eve, Eleanor expels Val into the snow and schedules the wedding for midnight, while Val books the 11:59 train out of Grand Central. Nate must choose between his inheritance and the woman he never stopped loving, and Val must prove the letters are real before the train leaves. The story pays off every planted object: the tin of letters, the forged letter, the crystal star ornament, the prenup, and the sealed letter. It ends with the star signature exposed, Eleanor unrepentant, Madison gone, and Val letting the midnight train leave so she can stay with Nate.
